Business Overview

Molbio Diagnostics, incorporated in 2000 and headquartered in Verna, South Goa, is a point-of-care (POC) molecular diagnostics company built around its proprietary Truenat platform — a portable, battery-operated, chip-based real-time PCR system that delivers results in roughly an hour without requiring lab infrastructure, stable power, or cold-chain reagents. The platform tests for 30+ infectious and non-communicable diseases (TB, COVID-19, HIV, Hepatitis B/C, HPV, dengue and others) and is deployed in 90+ countries. In 2020 the WHO endorsed Truenat assays as an initial test for TB detection and rifampicin resistance — a rare validation for an Indian-origin diagnostics platform, and one competitors cannot simply replicate on a press release.

The business runs a razor/razorblade model: Truenat and Trueprep devices are the capital good; disease-specific micro-PCR test kits are the recurring, higher-margin consumable. Six manufacturing facilities span Goa (2), Bengaluru (2), Visakhapatnam, and Pune, with installed device capacity of 5,400 units as of March 2026. Subsidiaries extend into digital radiology and digital pathology (following the 2023 acquisition of Prognosys Medical Systems). The company converted from private to public limited in January 2025 ahead of this listing.

Backed by Temasek Holdings (invested 2022 at a reported ~$1.5bn valuation) and Motilal Oswal Private Equity, alongside founder-promoters Sriram Natarajan (CEO, 35 years in diagnostics) and Dr. Chandrasekhar Bhaskaran Nair (CTO, 33 years in bioprocess/tech development).

Series Rationale — Why Category B

Revenue grew from ₹836.56 Cr (FY24) to ₹1,445.69 Cr (FY26), a ~31.4% 2-year CAGR — comfortably clearing the Cat B >25% threshold. The global point-of-care testing market (₹USD 29.3bn → USD 67.1bn by 2030, ~17.9% CAGR per RHP/1Lattice) is large and underpenetrated, and Molbio's patent estate is a genuine structural asset. However, Cat B also requires "FCF accelerating" — and FY26 operating cash flow fell to ₹50.39 Cr from ₹287.10 Cr in FY25, an 82% collapse in the same year PAT margin compressed from 13.48% to 11.28%. That is a direct contradiction of the Cat B FCF criterion. I am keeping the Cat B classification because the growth, TAM, and moat characteristics dominate, but this is flagged explicitly rather than smoothed over — it is the single most important number in this report and it drives the PASS verdict in Tab 6.

What Makes This One Interesting — and Difficult

Interesting: a real, hard-to-replicate technology moat (100+ country patents, WHO endorsement, first mover in India's TB elimination program) in a structurally growing global category, run by domain-expert founders with credible institutional backing.

Difficult: 84.56% of FY26 revenue comes from Indian government bodies and international aid agencies, and 70.20% comes from TB kits alone — a concentration that makes revenue hostage to public-health budget cycles and a single disease's testing algorithm. Layer on the OCF collapse, a debt jump (₹123 Cr → ₹413 Cr), and an IPO structure where 78.7% of proceeds are an OFS exit for existing shareholders (only ₹200 Cr of the ₹940 Cr enters the company) — and a 57–69x trailing P/E on Day 1 leaves very little room for anything to go wrong.
